# SEO and AEO discoverability

## When to use

Use for technical SEO, local SEO, AEO, public content clarity, crawlability,
indexability, metadata, canonicals, robots, sitemaps, and internal linking.

## When not to use

Do not use for general visual styling, JSON-LD implementation details, or
ranking promises and unsupported marketing claims.

## Inputs

The project profile, affected routes, public facts, canonical URL policy,
metadata source, navigation, sitemap/robots configuration, and search intent.

## Purpose

Make public content crawlable, unambiguous, semantically clear, factual, and
useful to people and answer systems. Discoverability is not a promise of
ranking, inclusion, or traffic.

## Process

1. Read the project profile and identify the real audience, service, location,
   entity, contact path, and source-backed claims.
2. Inspect route metadata, visible headings, links, structured data, canonical
   handling, robots, sitemap, and the actual rendering path.
3. Give important pages a specific title and description. Make the visible
   content answer, where applicable: WHO, WHAT, WHERE, SERVICES, and HOW TO
   CONTACT.
4. Improve semantic HTML, crawlable navigation, internal links, entity
   consistency, and search-intent coverage before adding copy volume.
5. For local pages, keep service area, business identity, contact details, and
   location language consistent with the project facts. Do not fill gaps with
   guesses.
6. Treat `llms.txt` as optional and experimental. It never replaces HTML,
   metadata, sitemap, robots, canonical handling, or structured data.

## Forbidden shortcuts

No keyword stuffing, hidden text, fake local presence, fabricated reviews,
unverified awards, invented addresses, or ranking guarantees. AEO means making
truthful public information legible and citeable, not gaming an answer system.

## Progressive disclosure

- Read [local-seo.md](references/local-seo.md) for location and entity checks.
- Read [aeo-content.md](references/aeo-content.md) for public answer-oriented
  content structure and claim discipline.

## Done criteria

- Metadata, canonical, crawlability, and navigation are checked for the scope.
- Visible content and entities are clear and source-backed.
- Local claims are consistent or explicitly marked unknown.
- Structured data is handled by `structured-data` rather than duplicated here.

## Evidence

Record source-backed claims, route metadata, visible headings, canonical and
crawlability checks, internal-link changes, and limitations on discoverability.

## Stop / escalation conditions

Stop when a requested SEO claim lacks a source, a local detail is ambiguous, or
the request asks for hidden text, keyword stuffing, fake reviews, or ranking
guarantees.
